# Chains — detailed master prompt, draft v2

This authoring master describes the complete intended behaviour. It exceeds the current provider's 5,000-character request limit. Use the four complete runtime prompts in `chains-runtime-v2.json` for local evaluation; do not append the long live global prompt to them. No database prompt or production routing is changed by these files.

## Reference interpretation and output order

| Image slot | Reference treatment | Chain presentation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | White architectural steps | Clean full-product drape across broad white steps |
| 2 | Charcoal curved display | Full-product elongated curve over a matte curved support |
| 3 | Emerald velvet rolls | Full-product drape across soft horizontal velvet rolls |
| 4 | Burgundy geometric blocks | Full-product U or open curve against a saturated block arrangement |

The references establish background, support, palette, camera and lighting direction. Their chain designs, product counts, logos, flowers, dried leaves, loose rocks and decorative fans are not product instructions. The source product photograph establishes jewellery identity and quantity.

Produce the first N separate images in the above order: one image uses scene 1; two use scenes 1 and 2; three use scenes 1–3; four use all four. All four prompts must work independently from the same raw photograph. Never return a four-panel board, contact sheet, composite, before/after comparison or collage. Each request produces exactly one square photograph.

## Shared instructions — apply to every scene

### 1. Purpose and instruction priority

Transform the wholesaler's raw photograph of a standalone neck chain into a premium JewelIndia studio product photograph. Improve exposure, light, colour accuracy, clarity, environment and physically plausible placement. Preserve the actual product.

Priority: source identity and construction; actual jewellery quantity and components; complete commercial-material removal without jewellery loss; physical continuity and gravity; source-supported detail; complete product readability; photographic quality; reference styling.

When a scene cannot be achieved without changing the chain, adapt the display dimensions, camera distance or pose rather than changing its construction. When an image is unclear, preserve uncertainty rather than filling it with invented craftsmanship. A attractive photograph of a different chain fails.

### 2. Identify the true product before styling

Distinguish real jewellery from price-tag strings, plastic ties, clips, paper, hands, packaging and source display hardware. Identify visible chain runs, interlocking links, solid segments, decorative stations, genuine clasps, terminal rings, end caps, connectors and any genuine additional components.

Determine the supported quantity from the source. Two sides of one loop are not two products; overlapping sections are not extra strands; a mirror reflection is not another chain. Do not interpret an obscured back section as evidence of additional jewellery. If the actual quantity cannot be established, preserve the visible arrangement and do not invent a grouped presentation.

This category is for standalone neck chains. Never add a pendant, charm, sacred black beads, central ornament or matching bracelet. If the supplied product contains a real unexpected pendant or component, preserve it rather than deleting jewellery for category conformity; that input needs category review outside generation.

### 3. Exact construction lock

Preserve source-supported physical length and proportions, link width and thickness, individual link shapes, link pitch, spacing, sequence, decorative-station order, strand count, clasp type, connector arrangement, metal colour, polished/matte contrast, engraving and handcrafted variation.

Treat each visible chain style according to its source construction, whether fine linked, rope-like, curb-like, box-like, woven, flat articulated, or alternating solid and openwork segments. These are examples, not a menu from which to replace the product. Do not choose a generic design when the source is difficult.

Do not replace open links with solid balls; turn filigree or openwork stations into smooth beads; turn a rope twist into a repeated chain of circles; flatten a broad articulated chain into a ribbon; add decorative spacers; change pattern rhythm; exaggerate thickness; or increase chain density to make the image look expensive.

Preserve the count and order of reliably visible repeated elements. Never extrapolate exact unseen link counts. Preserve actual asymmetric patterning and wear rather than automatically mirroring both sides.

### 4. Quantity, multiple products and overlapping runs

If the source contains one chain, show exactly one chain even when the reference contains several. If the source contains multiple genuine approved chains, retain exactly that count and each distinct design; scale the support arrangement to display the group. Do not create a second product merely to imitate reference composition.

Maintain strand count within each product. A double-strand product is not two independently interchangeable chains. Preserve shared terminals and source-supported joins. Keep overlapping runs spatially distinct with plausible depth order. No fused intersections, impossible crossings, detached ends or duplicated loops.

Do not transform source clutter into a new retail assortment. Other incidental products in the background are not automatically approved subjects.

### 5. Commercial removal without structural damage

Remove prices, stickers, tags, barcodes, SKU/inventory text, seller/store marks, watermarks, social handles, promotional lettering, paper cards and their identification strings or clips. Do not copy any reference brand name or add JewelIndia text as an image watermark.

Remove the complete commercial object and its non-jewellery attachments. Blurring or hiding a label is not removal. Genuine end rings, clasps and connector loops must remain. A fine jewellery chain must not be erased because it resembles a tag string.

Reconstruct exposed background only. If a tag covers a link or clasp, do not invent its underside, setting or mechanism. Preserve only visible supported jewellery and do not claim to recover hidden structure. When a source is incomplete, the full-product requirement means all supported visible structure, not manufactured missing ends.

### 6. Pose, articulation and total length

Product construction is fixed; photographic pose may change through genuine articulation. A flexible chain may form a broader or shallower curve, lie over a support or hang down its front without changing link dimensions, total physical length, connection order or component count. The silhouette of the photographic pose may change; the identity and geometry of its parts may not.

Never force a deep U simply because the reference has one. Never shorten a long chain to fit the square frame, pull an open chain into a closed loop, manufacture a clasp or stretch fine links. Adjust the support span and camera framing to the real chain.

Rigid engraved stations remain rigid. Flexible joins between stations articulate within plausible limits. Flat chains retain their width and cannot turn through impossible sharp bends. Twist-sensitive or delicate constructions need broader curves and smoother support edges.

For an open chain, preserve actual open ends and avoid inventing closure. For a closed chain, retain source-supported continuity without inventing invisible clasp details. Where the source pose or available information cannot support a radical repositioning, keep a pose closer to the source and adapt the scene around it.

### 7. Support, gravity and edge transitions

Every chain has real physical support. Top sections lie on the display, intermediate sections follow its contour or hang in a plausible free span, and lower sections form natural curves according to weight and available length.

At an edge, the chain rolls over the edge continuously rather than passing through a block. A hanging portion falls in the actual gravity direction. On an inclined surface, the chain must appear to rest against the support rather than cling to a vertical wall without support. Do not invent invisible hooks, welds or adhesive attachment.

Use broad, slightly softened display edges that protect readability and permit believable articulation. Do not allow sharp support corners to slice through links. Do not suspend long horizontal spans without tension or let a heavy section float.

### 8. Contact, shadows and negative space

Surface-contact shadows are small and attached to the actual points of contact. A suspended run may cast a projected shadow on a nearby display face; its shadow can be offset by the physical separation and light direction. It must not receive a fake contact outline as though glued to the face.

Preserve small open spaces within links. Show surface colour through openings where appropriate, not solid metal fills. Where runs overlap, use modest occlusion and depth separation. Do not add heavy black outlines to separate fine jewellery from the background.

On velvet, allow subtle pile compression and local settling. Do not bury fine links, fill openings with fibres, allow fabric through solid metal or wrap cloth around the chain. On hard blocks, preserve a stable clean surface and natural contact without artificial dents.

### 9. Metal, lighting and colour accuracy

Preserve the source-supported metal colour and finish. Gold stays metallic rather than flat yellow, neon orange or glossy plastic; silver, white gold, rose gold and mixed-metal products retain their supported appearance. Do not infer purity or material certification from colour.

Use controlled primary light, restrained fill and coherent reflections. Bright highlights follow individual curved and faceted surfaces; recessed or engraved portions retain readable tonal depth. Protect highlight detail on thin links and polished areas.

Background colour may cast a subtle plausible reflection, but emerald or burgundy surroundings must not recolour the entire chain. Use neutral fill so the product remains source-accurate. Do not add stones, synthetic sparkle, glowing edges or decorative bokeh dots that resemble new gems.

### 10. Camera, scale and framing

Each output is exactly 1:1, with one coherent camera view. Adapt the portrait reference treatments to a square by reframing the display, not stretching or compressing the product.

Keep the entire supported chain composition, including visible ends and clasp, inside the frame with modest breathing room. All four proposed scenes are full-product views; no macro crop is authorized in this draft. Prefer a product-oriented perspective without fisheye distortion or extreme foreshortening.

Keep chain-defining links, decorative stations, connectors and clasp sharp across the useful product plane. Focus stacking appearance is acceptable; excessive shallow depth of field that hides half the chain is not. Background may soften away from the product. Do not misrepresent physical scale by adding familiar objects or invented dimensions.

### 11. Source correction and thin-chain handling

Correct phone-photo noise, compression, unwanted colour casts and uneven exposure. Preserve thin links during segmentation and avoid alpha halos, jagged boundaries, erased openwork and thickened contours. Do not turn sharpening into fabricated engravings or artificial facets.

For a very fine chain, improve camera framing and controlled contrast instead of enlarging its links. For dark or reflective jewellery, use fill and background separation rather than bright painted outlines. For blurry input, preserve broad supported form without claiming recovered fine structure. A clipped or obstructed input does not authorize newly rendered missing jewellery.

### 12. Permitted styling and exclusions

Only the scene's named display geometry, surface material and palette are authorized. The chain is the hero. Do not reproduce logos, typography, decorative borders, hands, models, flowers, dried leaves, loose stones, fans, packaging, coins, perfume or additional jewellery from references.

A sparse background is intentional. Do not fill empty space by duplicating the product. Do not copy the reference chain's link design even if that design appears better suited to its support.

## Scene 1 — white architectural steps

Create a bright, premium studio photograph on two or three broad stepped plinths in matte warm-white or neutral-white material. Use clean rectangular forms, subtly softened edges and a continuous white studio background. No marble veining, glitter, decorative floor strip or printed text.

Arrange the actual source chain across the upper landing and over one broad step transition, with the lower run forming a relaxed supported curve on the landing below. For a closed chain, create a readable elongated loop with both sides traceable. For an open chain, use a gentle continuous open curve and retain actual ends. Choose plinth span and height to suit the product length. Do not copy the multiple-chain assortment shown in the reference.

Use an elevated three-quarter view that reveals top surfaces, front faces and edge transitions while keeping the whole chain in the square frame. Prefer a moderately directional upper-left key with controlled softness and gentle fill. Shadows may be clear and sculptural, but must preserve link readability. Projected shadows down a vertical face must agree with chain-to-face distance. Preserve detailed metallic highlights without blowing polished gold to white.

The result should feel architectural, bright and precise, with enough white negative space for clean product inspection. Prioritize full-chain readability over dramatic shadow length or a complicated staircase.

## Scene 2 — charcoal curved display

Create a restrained dark studio treatment using a broad matte charcoal curved or gently cylindrical display support over a continuous dark neutral surface. Use a subtly tactile, non-glossy finish; do not create a featureless black void or a shiny pipe.

Place the chain over the rounded support so the upper run rests on its crest and the lower runs follow the contour into a relaxed elongated loop on the supported foreground. Use an open curve when required by actual open ends. Match radius to articulation so wide or rigid segments do not wrap impossibly tightly. Keep source quantity: the reference contains two distinct chains, but a single-chain upload must remain a single product.

Use a modest elevated diagonal view with a controlled neutral upper-left key, restrained fill and enough tonal separation to read every source-supported link. Preserve fine openwork and solid decorative stations according to the source, not the reference's thick openwork chain designs. Exclude the printed seller mark, loose rocks and decorative fan. Keep the chain itself sharp and the background subordinate.

The result should feel sculptural and rich, with visible contour, coherent contact and reflective metal. Do not use near-black exposure that hides thin links or a bright halo that invents their boundary.

## Scene 3 — emerald velvet rolls

Create deep emerald/teal-green velvet with two or three broad horizontal upholstered rolls or soft ridges. The fabric should have believable short pile, subtle directional texture and restrained tonal variation. Keep it tactile rather than shiny satin or plastic.

Drape the actual chain over the upper roll and let the two runs descend over the soft contours into a relaxed lower curve resting on the foreground velvet. Preserve open ends if present. Use generous roll radii, adjust ridge height to product length, and prevent a fine chain from disappearing into a pile valley. The textile compresses subtly at contact; metal construction does not deform to match cloth.

Use a near-front view with a slight elevated angle, softly directional upper-left light and neutral fill. Keep the full chain sharp and visible inside the square. Allow controlled background softness without blurring connectors or the lower turn. Green reflections are slight and physically plausible; product colour remains source-accurate. Exclude the purple flowers and dried palm decoration visible in the reference.

The result should feel tactile and luxurious, with a clear continuous chain and realistic velvet contact. There must be no invented central pendant at the lower curve.

## Scene 4 — burgundy geometric blocks

Create a monochromatic deep burgundy studio environment with one primary wine/magenta display block and a restrained secondary block if needed for depth. Use matte or fine satin surfaces, crisp architectural planes and gently softened edges. Keep the palette controlled and avoid fluorescent pink, extreme gloss or a distracting assortment of platforms.

Support the actual chain over the primary block's top edge. Let its two sides descend naturally in front, forming a source-compatible lower curve. Each hanging section must remain connected to a supported top run; it cannot stick unsupported to a vertical wall. Use an open curve for open construction. Adapt block height and width to preserve real product length and full visibility. Do not create the reference's second chain when only one source chain exists.

Use a front-oriented camera with enough elevated perspective to show the top support and a little block depth. Apply controlled upper-left directional light, moderate fill and restrained side separation. Keep gold highlights legible against the saturated background without turning metal pink. Distinguish projected shadows from contact shadows. Keep the complete chain in a square frame; do not reserve the large empty portrait header from the reference at the expense of product scale.

The result should feel graphic and premium while remaining a believable photograph of a chain on a display. Exclude the reference logo and all typography.

## Final inspection for every generated image

Check source against output, not output against reference jewellery:

- Exact approved product count; no assortment invented from the reference.
- Correct link construction, repeated sequence, width, thickness, decorative stations and strand count.
- Visible clasp, end fittings and connections preserved; hidden structures not guessed.
- No added pendant, extra loop, charm, bead, gemstone or copied reference design.
- Source-supported complete chain visible, with no shortening or stretching to fit the square.
- Natural articulation, supported top runs and gravity-respecting lower sections.
- No fused links, impossible crossings, missing connectors or erased openwork.
- Contact and projected shadows correspond to actual physical support and light direction.
- Source-accurate metal tone, coherent reflections and controlled highlights.
- Sharp useful product details, without fabricated sharpening detail or background-removal halos.
- Commercial tags, strings, branding and watermarks fully removed without actual jewellery loss.
- Only the selected scene's permitted background and support; no imported reference clutter.
- Exactly one square photograph, no collage and no text.

If a check fails, correct the photograph or simplify the staging. Do not redesign the product to make the scene work.
